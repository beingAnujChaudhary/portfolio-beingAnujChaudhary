# German A1 – Lecture 1: Pronouns, Verb Conjugation & Simple Sentences

**Reference:** *Learn German in Hindi (Level A1, Episode 1) by Kedar Jadhav*

## 1. Introduction: Why Learn German Through Hindi? (जर्मन हिंदी के माध्यम से क्यों सीखें?)

Most students in India learn German through English, which often makes German grammar feel complicated. However, **German and Hindi share deep grammatical similarities** that English lacks:

1. **Gendered Possessives:** Just as Hindi has *मेरा* / *मेरी / मेरे* (unlike English, which only has *my*), German changes words based on gender.

2. **Informal vs. Respectful "You":** English uses *"You"* for everyone. Both Hindi and German distinguish between informal singular (**तू** = `du`), informal plural (**तुम सब / तुम दोनों** = `ihr`), and formal/respectful (**आप** = `Sie`).

3. **Verb Conjugation System:** Both Hindi and German conjugate verbs by removing the infinitive ending (**-ना** in Hindi; **-en** in German) to find the root/stem word, and then attaching a fixed suffix based on the subject pronoun.

## 2. German Personal Pronouns (जर्मन सर्वनाम – Personalpronomen)

To build sentences in German, you first need to master these core pronouns:

| German Pronoun | Hindi Meaning | English Meaning | Usage & Notes | 
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **ich** | मैं | I | First-person singular | 
| **du** | तू / तुम (एकवचन) | You (informal singular) | Used with friends, family, children | 
| **er** | वह / यह (पुल्लिंग) | He | Masculine singular | 
| **sie** | वह / यह (स्त्रीलिंग) | She | Feminine singular (lowercase `s`) | 
| **es** | वह / यह (नपुंसकलिंग) | It | Neuter singular / objects | 
| **ihr** | तुम सब / तुम दोनों | You all (informal plural) | Addressing 2+ informal people | 
| **wir** | हम | We | First-person plural | 
| **sie** | वे / वे लोग | They | Third-person plural (lowercase `s`) | 
| **Sie** | आप | YOU (formal) | Respectful singular or plural (always Capital `S`) | 

### Important Note on `sie` vs. `Sie` (Context Rule)

* **Small `sie` (वह - She)** vs. **Small `sie` (वे - They):** Just as English *"you"* can mean one person (*"You are Kedar"*) or many people (*"You are students"*), and Hindi *"कल"* can mean *yesterday* or *tomorrow* depending on the verb, German uses context and the verb ending to tell you whether `sie` means **She** (`sie trinkt`) or **They** (`sie trinken`).

* **Capital `Sie` (आप - Formal You):** Always written with a capital **S**, representing respect just like **आप** in Hindi.

## 3. Verb Conjugation (क्रिया रूप परिवर्तन – Verbkonjugation)

### Step A: Understand the Hindi Logic First

In Hindi, almost every action verb ends with **-ना** (खाना, पीना, आना, जाना, खेलना).

1. Take the verb **पीना** (to drink).

2. Remove **-ना** to get the **Stem / Root Word (मूल शब्द)**: **पी**.

3. Attach the ending according to the person:

   * मैं + पी + **ता हूँ** = मैं पीता हूँ

   * तू + पी + **ता है** = तू पीता है

   * हम + पी + **ते हैं** = हम पीते हैं

### Step B: Apply the Exact Same Logic in German

In German, **99% of verbs end with `-en`** (e.g., *kommen, gehen, lernen, essen, trinken, spielen*).

1. Take the verb **trinken** (पीना / to drink).

2. Remove **-en** to get the **Stem / Root Word**: **trink**.

3. Add the fixed German ending based on the pronoun:

| Pronoun | Ending Rule | Root + Ending | Complete Form | Hindi Meaning | English Meaning | 
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **ich** | add **-e** | trink + **e** | **ich trinke** | मैं पीता/पीती हूँ | I drink | 
| **du** | add **-st** | trink + **st** | **du trinkst** | तू पीता/पीती है | You drink | 
| **er / sie / es** | add **-t** | trink + **t** | **er/sie/es trinkt** | वह पीता/पीती है | He/She/It drinks | 
| **ihr** | add **-t** | trink + **t** | **ihr trinkt** | तुम सब पीते हो | You all drink | 
| **wir** | add **-en** | trink + **en** | **wir trinken** | हम पीते हैं | We drink | 
| **sie / Sie** | add **-en** | trink + **en** | **sie/Sie trinken** | वे पीते हैं / आप पीते हैं | They / YOU drink | 

> **The Conjugation Mantra (कंठस्थ करने का मंत्र):**
>
> * **Endings:** `-e`, `-st`, `-t`, `-t`, `-en`, `-en`
>
> * **Chant:** *ich trinke, du trinkst, er/sie/es trinkt, ihr trinkt, wir trinken, sie/Sie trinken!*
>
> * **Shortcut for `wir`, `sie` (they), and `Sie` (आप):** Because you remove `-en` and add `-en` back, the verb looks identical to the original infinitive form (*trinken*)!

## 4. Making Simple Sentences: The "Verb in 2nd Position" Rule

### Word Order Difference: Indian vs. European Languages

* **Hindi / Marathi (Indian Languages):** The verb goes at the **end** of the sentence.

  * Example: *मैं (1) क्रिकेट (2) **खेलता हूँ (3 - Verb)**.*

* **German / English / Spanish (European Languages):** The conjugated verb **ALWAYS comes in the 2nd Position**.

  * English: *I (1) **drink (2 - Verb)** tea (3).*

  * Spanish: *Yo (1) **bebo (2 - Verb)** té (3).*

  * German: *Ich (1) **trinke (2 - Verb)** Tee (3).*

### 100% Correct German Sentences from Lecture 1

Using universal words (*Pepsi, Pizza, Cricket, Tee, Hindi*) that require no extra translation:

1. **With `trinken` (पीना - to drink) \[Stem: `trink`\]:**

   * *Ich trinke Tee.* (मैं चाय पीता हूँ / I drink tea.)

   * *Ich trinke Pepsi.* (मैं पेप्सी पीता हूँ / I drink Pepsi.)

   * *Du trinkst Pepsi.* (तू पेप्सी पीता है / You drink Pepsi.)

   * *Er trinkt Pepsi.* (वह पेप्सी पीता है / He drinks Pepsi.)

   * *Wir trinken Pepsi.* (हम पेप्सी पीते हैं / We drink Pepsi.)

   * *Sie trinken Pepsi.* (आप पेप्सी पीते हैं / वे पेप्सी पीते हैं।)

2. **With `spielen` (खेलना - to play) \[Stem: `spiel`\]:**

   * *Ich spiele Cricket.* (मैं क्रिकेट खेलता हूँ / I play cricket.)

   * *Du* spielst *Cricket.* (तू क्रिकेट खेलता है / You play cricket.)

   * *Er spielt Cricket.* (वह क्रिकेट खेलता है / He plays cricket.)

   * *Wir spielen Cricket.* (हम क्रिकेट खेलते हैं / We play cricket.)

3. **With `lernen` (सीखना - to learn) \[Stem: `lern`\]:**

   * *Ich lerne Hindi.* (मैं हिंदी सीखता हूँ / I learn Hindi.)

   * *Du lernst Hindi.* (तू हिंदी सीखता है / You learn Hindi.)

   * *Wir lernen Hindi.* (हम हिंदी सीखते हैं / We learn Hindi.)

4. **With `essen` (खाना - to eat) \[Stem: `ess`\]:**

   * *Ich esse Pizza.* (मैं पिज़्ज़ा खाता हूँ / I eat pizza.)

   * *Wir essen Pizza.* (हम पिज़्ज़ा खाते हैं / We eat pizza.)

* As Kedar Sir mentions in the lecture, **over 90% of German verbs** follow this exact regular conjugation pattern.

* *(Preview* for future lessons: A few verbs on the list—such as `essen, sprechen, sehen, laufen, lesen, fahren, treffen, schlafen`—are *irregular only in the `du` and `er/sie/es` forms, and stems ending in `-t` or `-d` like `arbeiten, antworten, baden, finden` add a helper `-e-` before `-st` and `-t` for easier pronunciation, e.g., `du arbeitest, er arbeitet`. ## 6. Kedar Sir's Golden Study Strategy (हर रोज़ 5 वाक्य बनाने का नियम)

1. **Write 5 New Sentences Every Day:** Maintain a dedicated notebook and write 5 simple sentences daily using the 30 verbs. Consistency for 3 months leads to fluency.

2. **Days 1–4 Focus:** Build sentences primarily with **`ich`** (मैं) and **`wir`** (हम) first so your brain builds instant confidence. Then expand to **`du`**, **`er/sie/es`**, **`ihr`**, and **`sie/Sie`**.

3. **Use English Filler Words When Needed:** Since this is Lecture 1 and you don't know all German nouns/prepositions yet, don't let vocabulary stop you from practicing verb conjugation!

   * *Ich gehe to office.* (Later you will replace *"to office"* with German)

   * *Ich komme from office.*

   * *Wir spielen Cricket.* (100% pure German!)
