The Two Most Essential Verbs: haben & sein

These are the most frequently used verbs in German. They are completely irregular — their forms cannot be predicted from the regular rules. You must memorize them. But once you do, enormous doors open: they form perfect tenses, passive voice, and countless expressions.

⚠️
Irregular Verbs
Both haben and sein are highly irregular. Do not try to derive their forms from the regular -en pattern. Memorize them as they are — you'll use them in every German conversation!

Haben vs Sein Overview

flowchart TD A[Core Irregular Verbs] --> B[haben (to have)] A --> C[sein (to be)] B -.-> D[Possession: Ich habe ein Auto] B -.-> E[States: Ich habe Angst] C -.-> F[Identity: Ich bin Student] C -.-> G[Description: Es ist kalt] C -.-> H[Location: Wir sind hier]

haben — to have (conjugation)

SubjectFormExample sentence
ichhabeIch habe einen Laptop. — I have a laptop.
duhastDu hast Zeit. — You have time.
er/sie/eshatEr hat Hunger. — He is hungry.
wirhabenWir haben eine Idee. — We have an idea.
ihrhabtIhr habt Glück. — You all are lucky.
sie/SiehabenSie haben Recht. — They/You are right.
💡
When to use haben
• Possession: Ich habe ein Auto. (I have a car.)
• Abstract states: Ich habe Angst. (I am afraid. / lit. "I have fear.")
• Fixed expressions: Hunger haben (to be hungry), Durst haben (to be thirsty), Recht haben (to be right)

sein — to be (conjugation)

SubjectFormExample sentence
ichbinIch bin Anuj. — I am Anuj.
dubistDu bist Student. — You are a student.
er/sie/esistDas Buch ist interessant. — The book is interesting.
wirsindWir sind in Delhi. — We are in Delhi.
ihrseidIhr seid müde. — You all are tired.
sie/SiesindSie sind freundlich. — They/You are friendly.
💡
When to use sein
• Identity: Ich bin Lehrer. (I am a teacher.)
• Description: Das Wetter ist schön. (The weather is nice.)
• Location: Wir sind in Berlin. (We are in Berlin.)
• Origin: Ich bin aus Indien. (I am from India.)
🧠
Memory Trick for sein
The forms bin, bist, ist, sind, seid, sind look very different from each other — even English has "am/is/are/was/were." Just think of them as a family with different nicknames: bin → bist → ist → sind → seid → sind. Notice wir and sie/Sie both use sind!

A Personalized German Self-Introduction

Here is an A1-level introduction using sein, haben, and verbs from this course:

Hallo! Ich heiße Anuj.
Ich komme aus Aligarh und wohne in Indien.
Ich arbeite bei British Airways.
Ich studiere Data Science und lerne Deutsch.
Ich habe viel Interesse an Sprachen.
Ich bin sehr motiviert!
✏️
Useful Phrases for Introductions
Ich heiße ___. — My name is ___ (lit. "I am called ___")
Ich komme aus ___. — I come from ___
Ich wohne in ___. — I live in ___
Ich bin ___ Jahre alt. — I am ___ years old.
Ich bin von Beruf ___. — My profession is ___
Ich spreche ___. — I speak ___

Forming Feminine Person Nouns with -in

Many German nouns referring to male people form a feminine counterpart by adding -in to the end. Sometimes the vowel also changes to an umlaut.

Masculine (der)Feminine (die)MeaningNote
der Freunddie Freundinfriend (m/f)Regular
der Lehrerdie Lehrerinteacher (m/f)Regular
der Studentdie Studentinstudent (m/f)Regular
der Arztdie Ärztindoctor (m/f)Vowel umlaut: a → ä
der Kochdie Köchincook (m/f)Vowel umlaut: o → ö
📖
Plural of Feminine Nouns
Feminine nouns ending in -in take -nen in the plural:
→ die Studentin → die Studentinnen
→ die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen
