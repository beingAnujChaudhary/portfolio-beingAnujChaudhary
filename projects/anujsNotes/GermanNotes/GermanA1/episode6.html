Greetings and Polite Expressions

First impressions matter! German has formal and informal greeting systems. Using the right one shows cultural awareness and respect.

Time-based greetings

GermanEnglishWhen to use
Guten Morgen!Good morning!From waking up until ~10–11 am
Guten Tag!Good day!From ~11 am until ~6 pm (also formal "hello")
Guten Abend!Good evening!From ~6 pm onwards
Gute Nacht!Good night!When leaving for bed (NOT a hello!)
Hallo!Hello!Informal at any time
Hi!Hi!Very casual, among friends/young people

Essential polite phrases

GermanEnglishNote
Entschuldigung!Excuse me! / Sorry!Used to get attention or apologize
Entschuldigen Sie!Excuse me! (formal)Formal version

💡
Bitte — one word, many meanings!
Bitte can mean:
• "Please" (when requesting something): Ein Wasser, bitte!
• "You're welcome" (after danke): "Danke!" — "Bitte!"
• "Here you go" (when handing something): Bitte sehr!
• "Excuse me? / Pardon?" (when you didn't hear): Bitte?

Modal Verbs (Modalverben)

Modal verbs change or modify the meaning of another (main) verb. The modal takes position 2 (conjugated), and the main verb goes to the end of the sentence as an infinitive.

📖
Modal Verb Sentence Structure
Subject + Modal (conjugated) + … + Main Verb (infinitive)

✅ Ich kann gut Deutsch sprechen. — I can speak German well.
✅ Du musst das Buch lesen. — You must read the book.
✅ Wir wollen nach Berlin fahren. — We want to drive to Berlin.

The 6 core modal verbs — meaning & nuance

ModalCore meaningKey nuanceExample
könnencan / be able toAbility, possibilityIch kann schwimmen. — I can swim.
müssenmust / have toNecessity, obligationIch muss arbeiten. — I have to work.
dürfenmay / be allowed toPermissionDarf ich fragen? — May I ask?
sollenshould / supposed toInstruction, expectationDu sollst lernen. — You are supposed to study.
wollenwant toStrong intentionWir wollen reisen. — We want to travel.
mögento likePreference (often with nouns)Ich mag Kaffee. — I like coffee.
möchtenwould like toPolite wish / requestIch möchte Tee, bitte. — I would like tea, please.

Key Pattern for Modal Verbs
• ich and er/sie/es have the same form (no -e, no -t ending)
• Most lose their umlaut in singular: können → ich kann
• wir and sie/Sie are identical to the infinitive

This is the opposite of normal verbs, where ich and er are different!
⚠️
mögen vs möchten
mögen = to generally like something: Ich mag Pizza. (general preference)
möchten = would like (polite, present request): Ich möchte Pizza. (right now, please)

Use möchten in restaurants and polite requests — it sounds much more natural and polite!
